          The idea in one minute

          A planning assistant built for your land

          On a hillside like yours, what a lot is worth depends mostly on the valley view, how steep the ground is, and where the lot sits. Ordinary planning tools ignore the view. They also treat leased reserve land as if it were ordinary land that is bought outright. So plans for land like this are drawn by hand, and there is no solid way to show that a layout makes the most of the site.

          Over 16 weeks I will build a tool that fixes this. It studies your land in 3D. It learns from Sun Rivers, Sienna Ridge and nearby neighbourhoods what views and slopes are actually worth. Then it automatically designs and compares many possible layouts for the community.

          At the end you get the best plan, the numbers behind it, 3D and photo-style images, and a tool you own and can keep using.

          The proof

          Every accuracy check and comparison in one place, including how the plan compares with Sienna Ridge, with the limitations stated openly.

          The handover

          Final report and presentation

          A final presentation to you, plus a report and instructions so the tool stays useful after the project ends.

          Week by week

          Exactly what happens each week

          Each week shows what I will be doing, what you will be able to see at the end of it, and why it takes the time it does. Tap a week to open it. Weeks marked Your part are where your input is needed.

          1

          Read the land

          Weeks 1–4 ·

          Build an accurate 3D model of the ten acres, and map where the best views are and where building is practical.

          Milestone 1: a checked site and view map of your land.

          Week 1

          • Meet with you to confirm the goals, the kind of community you have in mind, and anything about the land I should respect or avoid.
          • Collect your drone photos of the parcel and the Sienna Ridge documents.
          • Download the province's free laser-scan elevation data (LidarBC) for your land, Sun Rivers, and the whole valley you look out over.
          • Start turning the drone photos into a detailed 3D model of the site.
          What you'll seeA short summary of what we agreed, and a checklist of everything collected.
          Why it takes the timeTurning thousands of photos into 3D takes hours of computer processing. Getting every piece of data now prevents delays in later stages.
          Your part: the kickoff meeting. Please share the drone photos and the Sienna Ridge files.
          Heavy week
          Week 2

          • Line up the drone model with the government scan, and measure how well they match. The match must fall within the scan's published accuracy, or it is redone.
          • Strip away trees and objects to get the bare ground, aiming for about one-metre detail.
          • Measure how steep every spot is, which way it faces, and where the ground dips or bulges.
          • Sort the land into three zones: easy to build (gentle, 15% slope or less), buildable with extra engineering (15–25%), and no-build (steeper than 25%, or unstable).
          What you'll seeA colour map of the land showing easy, possible and no-build zones, and how many acres fall into each.
          Why it takes the timeThis is the foundation. Every later number depends on this model being right, so each step is checked and measured.
          Very heavy week
          Week 3

          • From every spot on the land, calculate how much of the valley and river a person standing there can see.
          • Give the valley and river view the most weight, because that is what buyers here pay for.
          • Combine the view map and the building-zone map into one "opportunity map".
          • Record every setting, so the exact same method can be applied to Sun Rivers and Sienna Ridge later.
          What you'll seeA heat map of your ten acres with the best-view areas highlighted.
          Why it takes the timeIt means millions of sight-line calculations across the whole valley. The settings must be fixed and written down so later comparisons are fair.
          Heavy week
          Week 4 Milestone 1

          • Check the 3D model against the drone photos and known ground heights.
          • Test the view calculation at Sun Rivers next door, where we can see for ourselves which lots have views.
          • Measure how often the computer agrees with what is actually there, and look into every case where it doesn't.
          • Walk you through the maps.
          What you'll seeThe finished site and view map, with a one-page summary of how accurate it is.
          Why it takes the timeA map we can't trust is worse than no map. This week proves it works before everything else is built on it.
          Your part: about an hour reviewing the maps. Do the best-view areas match what you know of the land?
          Steady week
          2

          Learn what each lot is worth

          Weeks 5–8 ·

          Teach a computer model, using real nearby properties, how much the view, slope, size, position and lease terms add to a lot's value.

          Milestone 2: a lot value model checked against real sales.

          Week 5

          • Gather records for Sun Rivers, Sienna Ridge and nearby Kamloops hillside neighbourhoods: lot boundaries (ParcelMap BC), assessed values (BC Assessment), and local market trends (Interior REALTORS® and CREA).
          • Clean the records and match them lot by lot.
          • Adjust older values to today's market, so all the numbers are comparable.
          What you'll seeA clean table of comparable lots, with every source listed.
          Why it takes the timeSales records for reserve land are scarce and scattered across different sources. Finding them and cleaning them up is slow, careful work.
          Heavy week
          Week 6

          • Run the exact same view and slope measurements on every comparable lot, so they are judged by the same yardstick as your land.
          • Add each lot's size and its position in its community (edge or interior, how high up).
          • Record the lease details: the lease payment, the years remaining, and whether the lot sits under a headlease. Ordinary valuation tools ignore these.
          What you'll seeThe finished set of example lots the model will learn from, lease details included.
          Why it takes the timeThis is where leased reserve land is treated properly for the first time. Mainstream tools assume land that is bought outright.
          Heavy week
          Week 7

          • Train a modern prediction model, and two others to compare it against: a second machine-learning model and the traditional appraisal-style method.
          • Test it fairly: the model is always tested on neighbourhoods it never saw while learning, so it can't "cheat".
          • Measure how far off its estimates are, in dollars and in percent.
          • Show exactly how much the view adds to value. This is the key research question.
          What you'll seeA plain chart of what drives lot value here, with the view's share shown clearly.
          Why it takes the timeThis is the most technically demanding week of the stage. The fair-testing setup is what makes the numbers hold up when lenders or investors question them.
          Very heavy week
          Week 8 Milestone 2

          • Compare the model's estimates with actual sales.
          • Check the estimates against the Sienna Ridge 110-lot operating budget, a real picture of costs and revenue.
          • Check accuracy separately for the best-view lots and the steepest lots, so no blind spot hides in an average.
          • Walk you through the results.
          What you'll seeThe finished lot value model, with an accuracy summary.
          Why it takes the timeAn estimate is only useful if it lines up with the real market. This week is about earning that trust.
          Your part: about an hour reviewing. Do the values for lots you know feel right?
          Steady week
          Holiday break
          3

          Design the layouts

          Weeks 9–12 ·

          Build the engine that automatically designs, scores and ranks complete subdivision layouts for your ten acres.

          Milestone 3: recommended layouts, compared with Sienna Ridge and stress-tested.

          Week 9

          • Turn the planning rules into precise instructions the computer must follow: the property line, setbacks, minimum lot size and width, road width and maximum road steepness, the gate, amenities, and no lots on no-build ground.
          • Define what "best" means: the most total lot value and the right number of lots, with extra cost counted for building on steeper ground.
          • Decide how to compare fairly with Sienna Ridge, which is a different-sized property.
          What you'll seeA one-page "rulebook" for the design engine, for you to confirm.
          Why it takes the timeIf the rules are wrong, every design is wrong. Precision here saves weeks later.
          Your part: confirm your preferences on lot sizes, the gate and amenities.
          Heavy week
          Week 10

          • Build the engine that draws a complete layout: the loop road, the gate, lots along the road, and amenity spaces.
          • Make it "evolve": it creates hundreds of layouts, keeps the best, combines and adjusts them, and repeats over many rounds.
          • Have every layout scored automatically by the value model from Stage 2.
          What you'll seeThe first computer-generated layouts, drawn on a map of your land.
          Why it takes the timeThis is the hardest engineering in the project. Shapes, rules and scoring all have to work together, and fast enough to try thousands of designs.
          Very heavy week
          Week 11

          • Run the engine to produce thousands of layouts and rank them.
          • Show the trade-off between "most value" and "most lots" rather than a single answer, so the choice stays yours.
          • Compare the best layouts with the Sienna Ridge plan and Sun Rivers. The goal is to match or beat a plan drawn by hand.
          • Walk you through a shortlist.
          What you'll seeA shortlist of 3–5 layouts, each with its value, lot count, and comparison with Sienna Ridge.
          Why it takes the timeLarge computer runs, plus careful comparison work so the head-to-head with Sienna Ridge is fair.
          Your part: a 1–2 hour review. Do these layouts fit the community you want, and do they make commercial sense? Your comments become new rules for the engine.
          Heavy week
          Week 12 Milestone 3

          • Change the key assumptions (what counts as too steep, how much the view matters compared with the lot count) and see whether the recommended plan changes.
          • Build in your feedback from last week.
          • Write up how solid the recommendation is.
          What you'll seeThe recommended layouts, plus a "how sure are we" summary you can show lenders or investors.
          Why it takes the timeIt proves the recommendation comes from the land and the market, not from one lucky assumption.
          Steady week
          4

          Put it in your hands

          Weeks 13–16 ·

          Bring everything together in a tool you can use, show the plan in 3D, and deliver the master plan and reports.

          Milestone 4: everything delivered and handed over to Sanctum.

          Week 13

          • Build a secure web app where you can see your land and its view and building maps, generate and compare layouts side by side, and click any lot to see its estimated value.
          • Add simple controls, such as "favour views more" or "allow steeper lots", that show the effect on the layout, lot count and total value straight away.
          • Store everything in a private, secure database.
          What you'll seeA working studio you can open in your web browser.
          Why it takes the timeIt joins all three earlier stages into one tool that someone without technical training can use.
          Very heavy week
          Week 14

          • Build an interactive 3D model of the plan on the real terrain, which you can turn and look around.
          • Create photo-realistic images of what the community could look like.
          • Give you a hands-on demo of the studio.
          What you'll seeThe 3D model and the first photo-realistic images.
          Why it takes the timeRealistic 3D on real terrain is demanding work, and the images need several rounds of refinement.
          Your part: a 1–2 hour demo. Choose your preferred layout for the master plan, and tell me how the images and your drone photos may be used.
          Very heavy week
          Week 15

          • Produce the conceptual master plan: numbered lots, the road, the gate and amenities, with a lot-by-lot table of size, view, slope and estimated value.
          • Write the validation report, with every accuracy check, comparison and limitation in one place.
          • Write clear instructions so the tool can be rerun and maintained.
          What you'll seeA draft master plan and validation report for your review.
          Why it takes the timeThe documents have to stand on their own in front of the community, lenders and buyers.
          Heavy week
          Week 16 Milestone 4

          • Present the recommended master plan, the studio and the visuals to you.
          • Hand over the final report, the instructions, and the tool itself, set up under Sanctum's own accounts.
          • Return or delete my working copies of your private files, and complete the Mitacs survey.
          What you'll seeEverything delivered, and owned by Sanctum.
          Why it takes the timeA clean handover is what makes the tool useful after the project ends.
          Your part: the final presentation meeting.

          What I need from you

          Your input makes the plan yours

          • At the start: the drone photos of the parcel, and the Sienna Ridge documents (plan drawing, operating budget, headlease, homeowners corporation bylaws).
          • Early on: the exact parcel boundaries, the access points from Shuswap Road, any easements or servicing, and any places of cultural significance.
          • Your vision: the number or size of lots you imagine, the gate, amenities, and the feel of the community.
          • At each review: about an hour of your time, and honest feedback. Your comments become rules the design engine follows.
          Your land, your data

          How your information is protected

          • Your data and everything built from it stay under Sanctum's control, following the First Nations OCAP® principles (ownership, control, access, possession).
          • Your files are used only for this project and kept in secure, private storage.
          • Your private information is never put into outside AI services without your permission.
          • Nothing is presented or published without your review and consent.
          • At the end, the tool and its results are handed over to Sanctum, and my working copies of your files are returned or deleted.
          If you hear these terms: a plain-English guide
          LiDAR / LidarBC
          A laser scan of the land taken from an aircraft. BC publishes these free of charge.
          Elevation model
          A 3D height map of the bare ground.
          Viewshed
          Everything you can see from a given spot. A "view score" rates how good that view is.
          Slope classes
          Easy to build (15% slope or less), buildable with engineering (15–25%), no-build (over 25%).
          Comparables
          Real nearby lots whose values teach the model what features are worth.
          Machine-learning model
          A program that learns patterns (here, what drives lot value) from many examples.
          Fair testing (cross-validation)
          Checking the model on neighbourhoods it never saw while learning.
          Headlease
          The long master lease on a whole property, which each lot's lease sits under.
          Trade-off frontier
          The menu of best options between "most value" and "most lots". Choosing from it is up to you.
          Sensitivity check
          Changing the assumptions to make sure the answer doesn't depend on one guess.
          Studio
          Your private planning web app.
